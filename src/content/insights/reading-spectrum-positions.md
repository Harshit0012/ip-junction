When investors look at a mobile network operator, spectrum holdings are often the largest asset on the table. They are also easy to misread. A headline figure such as total megahertz held says little about what the spectrum can do for the business.

We have analyzed MNO spectrum holdings for private equity investors. These are the questions we start with.

## Which bands, and what job does each do?

Spectrum below 1 GHz travels far and penetrates buildings, which makes it the coverage layer. Mid-band spectrum, roughly 1 to 6 GHz, carries most 5G capacity. Millimeter-wave bands offer very high capacity over short distances. A portfolio strong in one layer and thin in another implies a specific network strategy and specific costs.

The trade-off is concrete. An operator without low-band spectrum has to cover the same area with more sites. When we developed an LTE rollout strategy for SaskTel without 700 MHz spectrum, the gap analysis was largely about what that missing layer meant for coverage, site count and timing.

## How deep are the holdings where it matters?

The common comparison unit is MHz-POPs: megahertz multiplied by the population covered by the licence. It is useful for comparing portfolios but hides two things. First, holdings concentrated in low-density areas inflate the count without adding much revenue potential. Second, contiguous blocks are worth more than the same amount of fragmented spectrum, because wide channels deliver better performance.

## What do the licences require?

Licence term, renewal expectations and buildout obligations all affect value. An unmet buildout deadline can put a licence at risk. Some spectrum is encumbered by incumbent users or by clearing obligations that delay use.

## Can the operator actually deploy it?

Spectrum value depends on deployment. That means device support for the band, available radio equipment, enough sites to use the capacity, and the capital to build. Spectrum that sits unused because the device ecosystem is immature is an option, not a cash-flowing asset.

## A practical checklist

- Map holdings by band and market, not just in total.
- Compare depth against peers in the markets that drive revenue.
- List every licence obligation and its deadline.
- Check deployment status against holdings, band by band.
- Ask what capital the plan assumes and whether the operator has it.

A spectrum position read this way says far more about an operator's next five years than the headline number does.
